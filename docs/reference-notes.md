# Look reference notes

What real photographs show, and what Summit should match. Studied from Wikimedia
Commons featured pictures: Watzmann (west side), Julian Alps from Vršič Pass,
Mount Rainier aerial, Passo Pordoi (Dolomites), Tennfjorden (Norway), Grand
Canyon from Pima Point, karst peaks at Vang Vieng (Laos), Robson Valley
(Canada).

## Depth and air

- Aerial perspective is the strongest depth cue. Ridges a few kilometres away are
  already bluer and lower in contrast; at 10 km they are near-silhouettes in
  sky colour. Haze is thicker low down and in valleys.
- Shadows on snow and distant rock are clearly blue (sky light).

## Rock

- Steep faces carry dense, branching gullies and ribs at every scale, down to
  a few metres. Without this, mountains look like clay.
- Limestone is pale warm grey to beige, Dolomite walls go orange in sun.
  Granite and gneiss are mid grey. Lit faces are bright, not dark grey.
- Walls show vertical fluting (water streaks) plus horizontal ledges.
- Scree cones and fans at the foot of walls are paler than the wall itself
  (fresh broken rock).

## Snow

- Smooth and unbroken on gentle ground; rock shows only on steep bands.
- A light snowfall sticks to every ledge and bedding plane, so it draws the
  structure of the face in fine stripes.
- Snow fills chutes and couloirs well below the main snowline.

## Vegetation

- Sunlit alpine grass is saturated yellow-green, right up to the cliff foot,
  with grey boulders scattered through it.
- Forest climbs in fingers along ridges and gullies; the tree line is ragged.
- From a distance a forest is a continuous lumpy canopy: bright sunlit crown
  tops, dark gaps, no visible ground. Conifers are narrow spires with lighter
  tips; never black, even in flat light.

## Desert and canyon

- A layer cake: thin steep cliff bands alternating with gentler talus slopes.
- Only the cliff bands are vividly red, orange or cream. Talus and benches are
  muted tan and olive, dotted with dark scrub.

## Water

- Calm water is a near-perfect mirror of the sky and the mountains.
